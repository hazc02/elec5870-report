\chapter{Baseline Electrical Characterisation}
\label{ch:baseline-electrical}

This chapter presents the electrical characterisation of the depletion-mode
GaAs/AlGaAs HEMT devices fabricated using the unrecessed process described
in Chapter~\ref{ch:baseline-process}. The primary objective is to establish
a well-understood electrical baseline for threshold voltage, transconductance,
and gate leakage behaviour against which the effects of controlled gate
recession may be assessed.

\vspace{2mm}

Although devices of multiple lateral geometries were fabricated and
characterised, these variations are not treated as primary research variables.
The range of gate lengths and source--drain separations instead serves to
confirm that key electrostatic parameters --- most notably threshold voltage ---
are governed by the vertical heterostructure and Schottky gate properties
rather than by lateral device dimensions. This distinction is central to the
subsequent investigation of enhancement-mode operation, where threshold voltage
is expected to be strongly dependent on recess depth.

\section{Measurement Setup}
\label{sec:measurement-setup}

Electrical measurements were carried out using a Keysight B1500A
semiconductor device parameter analyser under ambient laboratory conditions at
room temperature (\SI{20}{\degree C}) \cite{leedscleanroom_equipment}.

\vspace{2mm}

The probe station is fitted with a halogen bulb above the device under test.
The bulb intensity can be varied, which would influence measured drain current,
threshold voltage, and gate leakage. All electrical measurements reported in
this work were therefore taken with the bulb switched off.

\vspace{2mm}

Output characteristics were measured by sweeping the drain--source voltage
$V_{DS}$ from \SI{0}{\volt} to \SI{5}{\volt} at fixed gate biases. Five curves
were acquired for each device, corresponding to gate voltages from
\SI{-1.2}{\volt} to \SI{0}{\volt} in \SI{0.3}{\volt} increments. These
measurements were used to assess channel conduction, low-bias linearity, and
current saturation behaviour.

\vspace{2mm}

Gate transfer characteristics were obtained by sweeping the gate voltage
$V_G$ from \SI{-3}{\volt} to \SI{0}{\volt} at a fixed drain bias of
\SI{5}{\volt}, chosen to ensure operation in the saturation regime. From these
measurements, the drain current $I_D$, threshold voltage $V_{th}$, and
transconductance $g_m$ were extracted. Gate leakage was also recorded during
the $V_{DS}$ sweeps at the same fixed gate biases used for the output
characteristics.

\vspace{2mm}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.45\linewidth]{figures/fabrication_images/probing.jpg}
  \hspace{2mm}
  \includegraphics[width=0.45\linewidth]{figures/fabrication_images/probes.jpg}
  \caption{Probe station setup used for electrical characterisation of
  fabricated devices.}
  \label{fig:probing_setup}
\end{figure}

\vspace{2mm}

Transconductance was calculated numerically from the measured
$I_D$--$V_G$ data using finite-difference differentiation. This gives the local
slope $dI_D/dV_G$ at fixed drain bias, with central-difference estimates used
for interior data points and one-sided differences at the ends of the sweep.
The reported scalar transconductance metric is the maximum absolute value of
the resulting $g_m(V_G)$ curve.

\vspace{2mm}

Threshold voltage was extracted from the transfer characteristics using a
constant-current method. The primary threshold current was set to
\SI{1}{\micro\ampere}, and the corresponding gate voltage was obtained by
linear interpolation between the two data points surrounding the current
crossing. Where no valid crossing was found at this level, a fallback criterion
of 1\% of the maximum absolute drain current was used, again with linear
interpolation. This procedure provided a consistent device-by-device
threshold-voltage metric, while the averaged transfer curves were used for
visual comparison of the overall turn-on behaviour.

\vspace{2mm}

Electrical characteristics were averaged across the three nominally identical
devices within each column to obtain representative behaviour for each
geometry. Arithmetic means were used for all plotted averages, while the spread
between repeats was retained for later discussion of device repeatability and
its implications for recess-process sensitivity.

\vspace{2mm}

\section{Baseline Output Characteristics}

The output characteristics of the fabricated depletion-mode GaAs/AlGaAs HEMTs
were examined to verify ohmic contact behaviour and confirm stable channel
conduction prior to threshold-voltage analysis.
Figure~\ref{fig:id_vds} shows the measured $I_D$--$V_{DS}$ characteristics for
a representative device with a gate length of \SI{6}{\micro\metre}, averaged
across the three nominally identical devices fabricated at this geometry.

\vspace{2mm}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.75\linewidth]{figures/electrical_analysis/output_characteristics.png}
    \caption{Measured output characteristics ($I_D$--$V_{DS}$) of a
    representative depletion-mode GaAs/AlGaAs HEMT with a \SI{6}{\micro\metre}
    gate length. Curves are shown for multiple gate voltages, illustrating
    linear-region conduction and current saturation.}
    \label{fig:id_vds}
\end{figure}

\vspace{2mm}

At low drain--source voltages, $I_D$ exhibits a clear linear relationship
with $V_{DS}$ for all applied gate biases, confirming ohmic conduction and
low-resistance source and drain contacts. This indicates that the ohmic
metallisation and annealing process was successful, and that contact
resistance does not dominate the measured device behaviour.

\vspace{2mm}

As $V_{DS}$ is increased, the devices transition smoothly into current
saturation, consistent with electrostatic pinch-off of the channel near the
drain. The systematic modulation of the saturation current with gate voltage
demonstrates effective gate control of the 2DEG and confirms stable operation.

\vspace{2mm}

\section{Gate Transfer Characteristics and Threshold Voltage}
\label{sec:baseline-transfer-characterstics}

The primary objective of this project is to understand and control threshold
voltage through gate recessing, so establishing the baseline threshold
behaviour of unrecessed devices is essential.

\vspace{2mm}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.75\linewidth]{figures/electrical_analysis/gate_transfer_average.png}
    \caption{Averaged transfer characteristics ($I_D$--$V_G$) for all
    fabricated depletion-mode HEMTs, comprising five lateral geometries with
    three nominally identical devices per geometry.}
    \label{fig:id_vg_all}
\end{figure}

\vspace{2mm}

Figure~\ref{fig:id_vg_all} shows the averaged linear-scale gate transfer
characteristics for the full device set. Across all geometries, the transition
from the depleted regime to strong channel conduction occurs at approximately
the same gate voltage, yielding a threshold voltage of
%
\[
V_T \approx \SI{-0.9}{\volt}.
\]
%
No systematic shift in the turn-on point is observed, demonstrating that
threshold voltage is effectively independent of lateral device geometry for
the unrecessed devices fabricated in this work.

\vspace{2mm}

This behaviour is consistent with Equation~\ref{eq:threshold_voltage}, in
which $V_T$ is governed by the vertical electrostatics beneath the Schottky
gate. With the gate formed on the GaAs cap, the depletion field must extend
through the cap, the full AlGaAs donor layer, and the undoped spacer to fully
deplete the 2DEG. Under these conditions $V_T$ depends on the Schottky
barrier height, donor charge density, and donor-layer thickness, while lateral
device dimensions do not appear in the first-order electrostatic model.

\vspace{2mm}

Differences observed in the on-state current between devices are modest and
are attributed to lateral transport effects, including variations in channel
and access resistance arising from differences in gate length and
source--drain separation. These variations influence current drive but do not
alter the electrostatic condition required to deplete the channel.

\vspace{2mm}

The key implication is that the baseline threshold voltage is set entirely by
the unrecessed vertical layer stack, yielding $V_T \approx \SI{-0.9}{\volt}$
across all fabricated geometries. This confirms that threshold-voltage
engineering must be achieved by modifying the vertical electrostatics beneath
the gate, and validates controlled gate recession into the AlGaAs donor layer
as the appropriate process variable for shifting $V_T$ into the positive regime.

\vspace{2mm}

\section{Transconductance}

Transconductance provides a measure of how effectively the gate modulates the
channel current at fixed drain bias, and is defined as

\vspace{2mm}

\begin{equation}
    g_m = \frac{dI_D}{dV_G}
\end{equation}

\vspace{2mm}

In this work, $g_m$ was extracted numerically from the measured
$I_D$ versus $V_G$ transfer characteristics at
$V_{DS}=\SI{5}{\volt}$ using the finite difference method described in
Section~\ref{sec:measurement-setup}. Figure~\ref{fig:gm_baseline} shows the
averaged transconductance characteristics for the five unrecessed device
geometries.

\vspace{2mm}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.78\linewidth]{figures/electrical_analysis/gm_baseline.png}
  \caption{Averaged transconductance characteristics for the unrecessed
  depletion mode GaAs/AlGaAs HEMTs, comprising five lateral geometries with
  three nominally identical devices per column.}
  \label{fig:gm_baseline}
\end{figure}

\vspace{2mm}

Across all columns, the transconductance remains close to zero through the
strongly pinched off region, before rising sharply as $V_G$ approaches the
$V_T$. This behaviour is consistent with the transfer
characteristics presented in Section~\ref{sec:baseline-transfer-characterstics}, where the
drain current begins to increase rapidly at approximately the same gate
voltage. The shared shape of the $g_m$ curves therefore confirms that all
devices exhibit the same underlying depletion mode switching behaviour. The
differences in peak magnitude, however, show that the strength of current
modulation depends on device geometry.

\vspace{2mm}

A first order theoretical guide is provided by the charge control expression
for the transconductance parameter $\beta$ introduced in Section~2.4.2:

\vspace{2mm}

\begin{equation*}
    \beta = \frac{\varepsilon \mu W}{(d + \Delta d)L}
\end{equation*}

\vspace{2mm}

For the unrecessed device set, the vertical layer structure is common to all
columns, so $\varepsilon$, $\mu$, and $(d+\Delta d)$ may be treated as
approximately fixed. Under this assumption, the model predicts that
$\beta \propto 1/L$. Shorter gate devices would therefore be expected to
exhibit stronger intrinsic gate control and, in turn, higher
transconductance. 

\vspace{2mm}

The measured data show a clear dependence of $g_m$ on device
geometry, but the trend is not determined by gate length alone. In particular,
the final two columns (Red and Purple Curves on Figure~\ref{fig:gm_baseline}) share the same nominal gate length of
\SI{4}{\micro\metre}, but exhibit different transconductance characteristics
because their ohmic separations are different. This indicates that the
measured transconductance is influenced not only by the intrinsic gate to
channel coupling captured by the charge control model, but also by lateral
access resistance and transport effects associated with the source to drain
geometry.

\vspace{2mm}

The key implication is that, unlike threshold voltage, transconductance is not
set solely by the vertical electrostatics beneath the gate. Instead, it
reflects both the intrinsic electrostatic coupling between the gate and the
channel, and the broader lateral transport conditions within the device.
Establishing this baseline behaviour is important for the recessed gate study
developed in later chapters, since any changes in transconductance must be
interpreted in the context of both recess induced electrostatic modification
and geometry dependent transport effects.

\vspace{2mm}

\section{Gate Leakage Behaviour}

Gate leakage was examined to characterise the gate--semiconductor interface
in the unrecessed devices and to provide a reference for comparison with
the recessed-gate devices characterised in Chapters~\ref{ch:iteration-1}
through~\ref{ch:iteration-3}.
Figure~\ref{fig:ig_vds} presents the averaged gate leakage characteristics
for all fabricated devices, spanning the full range of lateral geometries
within the array.

\vspace{2mm}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{figures/electrical_analysis/gate_leakage_average_bulb_off.png}
    \caption{Measured gate leakage characteristics for unrecessed
    depletion-mode GaAs/AlGaAs HEMTs, averaged across nominally identical
    devices for each column in the array (bulb off).}
    \label{fig:ig_vds}
\end{figure}

\vspace{2mm}

Across the full device set, measured gate leakage currents are of similar
order of magnitude over the applied drain-bias range, with no strong or
systematic dependence on gate length or source--drain separation. This
behaviour suggests that gate leakage is governed primarily by the
gate--semiconductor interface quality rather than by lateral device dimensions.

\vspace{2mm}

In the unrecessed geometry, the Schottky gate is formed on the GaAs cap layer,
where a thin native oxide and associated surface states are expected to be
present. The small spread in leakage observed between columns is therefore most
plausibly attributed to local variations in interface quality beneath the gate.

\vspace{2mm}

In recessed devices, the gate contacts the AlGaAs donor layer directly,
introducing a fundamentally different interface. Schottky contacts on AlGaAs
are commonly reported to exhibit increased leakage relative to GaAs-based
interfaces, reflecting the closer proximity of the donor layer to the gate,
a reduced effective barrier width, and enhanced tunnelling
currents. \cite{Schweeger1994} These effects are further influenced by
differences in surface-state density and band alignment at the
metal--semiconductor interface. \cite{Schweeger1994} The leakage behaviour
established here therefore provides a physically meaningful reference: any
systematic increase in gate leakage observed following recession can be
attributed to the modified interface rather than to pre-existing geometry
effects.

\section{Python-Based Data Processing Workflow}

As the number of electrical measurements increased throughout the project, a
Python-based analysis workflow was developed to process the exported
measurement files in a consistent and scalable manner. This reduced reliance
on manual spreadsheet handling and ensured that device metrics, averaging
procedures, and figure generation were applied uniformly across the full
dataset.

\vspace{2mm}

The workflow reads CSV files exported from the Keysight B1500A and
extracts device metadata from the filename and folder structure created during testing.
This includes the sample identifier, column, row, recess condition,
probe station bulb state, and measurement type. The processor was designed
to handle gate-transfer, output-characteristic, and channel-resistance
datasets, allowing a common analysis framework to be applied across multiple
electrical tests.

\vspace{2mm}

The pipeline calculates a range of useful device metrics, including transconductance, 
threshold voltage, and channel resistance using the extraction methods outlined earlier. 
It also aggregates repeated  measurements to produce averaged trends for nominally identical 
devices. In addition to analysing individual devices, the workflow supports direct comparison 
across different geometries, samples, recess conditions, and illumination states. This 
is particularly useful in the present study, as it provides a consistent framework 
for analysing the unrecessed devices in this chapter and later comparing them with 
the recessed devices introduced in subsequent chapters.
