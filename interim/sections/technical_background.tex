\section{Overview of GaAs/AlGaAs HEMTs}

In GaAs/AlGaAs high-electron-mobility transistors (HEMTs), the interface
between the GaAs channel layer and the overlying AlGaAs barrier produces
a discontinuity in the conduction band due to the differing bandgaps of
the two materials. This offset creates a potential well on the GaAs side
of the junction, as illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:band_bending}, which
confines electrons to the heterointerface and enables the formation of a
two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG).

Electrons supplied by silicon donors in the modulation-doped AlGaAs
layer transfer into this potential well and accumulate to form the
2DEG, which serves as the conducting channel of the device. The spatial
separation of carriers from ionised dopants results in exceptionally
high electron mobility, underpinning the strong transconductance,
low-noise performance, and high-frequency capability that distinguish
HEMTs from bulk-channel field-effect transistors \cite{liu1999}.

\section{The GaAs/AlGaAs Heterostructure}

The devices investigated in this project employ a conventional
GaAs/AlGaAs heterostructure in which a sequence of epitaxial layers is
engineered to create and control a 2DEG. A schematic cross-section of
the structure is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:layer_structure}, highlighting
the cap, donor, spacer, and channel layers whose thicknesses and
compositions govern the electrostatic control and transport behaviour of
the device.

\vspace{2mm}
\input{figures/fig-layer-structure.tex}
\vspace{5mm}

At the surface of the structure lies a $10~\text{nm}$ thick, highly doped
GaAs cap layer. This cap provides a chemically stable surface for
forming the Schottky gate contact whilst protecting the underlying
AlGaAs. Its high conductivity reduces series resistance in the access
regions adjacent to the source and drain contacts, improving carrier
injection into the channel.

\vspace{2mm}

Beneath the cap layer is a $40~\text{nm}$ thick silicon-doped AlGaAs
donor layer. Silicon donors act as electron suppliers, with carriers
transferring into the GaAs channel as a result of the conduction band
discontinuity at the heterointerface. This modulation-doping strategy
spatially separates the ionised dopants from the conducting channel.
Both the donor concentration and its thickness strongly influence the
resulting sheet carrier density of the 2DEG and therefore the threshold
voltage and maximum drain current of the device.

\vspace{2mm}

A $15~\text{nm}$ thick undoped AlGaAs spacer layer separates the donor
layer from the GaAs channel. This spacer suppresses Coulomb scattering
by physically isolating the 2DEG from ionised impurities, enabling
exceptionally high electron mobility \cite{Hirakawa1986}. The benefit is
particularly pronounced in HEMT devices operating at cryogenic
temperatures, where phonon scattering is reduced and impurity
scattering would otherwise dominate transport behaviour
\cite{Pfeiffer1989}. Increasing the spacer thickness improves mobility
but weakens electrostatic coupling between the gate and the channel;
this trade-off is a fundamental consideration in heterostructure
design.

\vspace{2mm}

In this project, the thickness of the doped AlGaAs donor layer beneath
the gate represents the primary controllable parameter through which
the threshold voltage may be engineered. The following section therefore
examines how gate bias modulates channel charge and determines the
operational mode of the device.

\section{Gate Control of Channel Charge and Threshold Voltage}

The electrical behaviour of the GaAs/AlGaAs HEMT is governed by the
field-effect interaction between the Schottky gate and the 2DEG. When a
metal gate is deposited onto the semiconductor surface, a depletion
region forms beneath it; the depth of this region is modulated by the
applied gate voltage ($V_G$). This modulation controls the sheet carrier
density ($n_s$) in the channel and, consequently, the drain current
\cite{sze2021}.

\subsection{Charge Distribution and Effective Gate Separation}

In a simple charge-control description of GaAs/AlGaAs HEMTs, the 2DEG is
treated as an infinitesimally thin sheet of charge located at the
GaAs/AlGaAs heterointerface, analogous to a parallel-plate capacitor; 
with the gate and channel acting as plates and the AlGaAs donor layer acting as a 'dielectric'. \cite{ivcv-hemt}
This approximation is adequate for devices with relatively thick
barrier layers. As the gate is recessed and the gate-to-channel
separation is reduced towards the nanometre scale required for
enhancement-mode operation, however, the assumption of a sheet-like
charge distribution becomes increasingly inaccurate.

\vspace{2mm}

In practice, electrons forming the 2DEG occupy a quantised triangular
potential well on the GaAs side of the heterointerface, resulting in a
charge distribution of finite thickness. The centre of this
distribution is displaced from the interface, increasing the effective
electrostatic separation between the gate and the channel.

\vspace{2mm}

Lee \textit{et al.} account for this behaviour by introducing an
effective gate-to-channel separation of $d + \Delta d$, where
$\Delta d$ represents a quantum-mechanical correction associated with
the finite spatial extent of the electron wavefunction. For
GaAs/AlGaAs HEMTs, $\Delta d$ is of order $80~\text{\AA}$
\cite{ivcv-hemt}. This correction may be incorporated into the
transconductance parameter $\beta$, which relates changes in gate
voltage to changes in drain current through the gate-controlled channel
capacitance:

\begin{equation}
    \beta = \frac{\epsilon \mu W}{(d + \Delta d)L}
\end{equation}

\begin{quote}
    \footnotesize
    \textit{$\epsilon$ : Permittivity of the Barrier; $\mu$ : Electron Mobility; 
    $W, L$ : Gate Width and Length; $d$ : Physical Gate-to-Channel Separation; 
    $\Delta d$ : Effective charge-centroid offset.}
\end{quote}

For the depletion-mode devices fabricated in Semester~1, the total
electrostatic separation between the gate and the 2DEG is sufficiently
large that this quantum correction represents a small fraction of the
overall spacing. As the donor layer is thinned towards the dimensions
required for enhancement-mode operation, the correction becomes a more
significant contribution. Its relevance in this project lies in
informing the practical recess depth required to shift the threshold
voltage to positive values, and an important consideration to the 
transconductance of the HEMT.

\subsection{Threshold Voltage and Mode Determination}

The distinction between depletion-mode (D-mode) and enhancement-mode
(E-mode) operation is determined by the balance between the built-in
potential of the Schottky gate and the thickness of the doped AlGaAs
donor layer. Within the analytical framework presented by Lee
\textit{et al.} \cite{ivcv-hemt}, the threshold voltage ($V_{off}$) may
be expressed as

\begin{equation}
    V_{off} = \phi_b - \Delta E_c - \frac{q N_d d_d^2}{2\epsilon}
    \label{equation:threshold_voltage}
\end{equation}

\begin{quote}
    \footnotesize
    \textit{$\phi_b$ : Schottky Barrier Height; $\Delta E_c$ : Conduction Band Offset; 
    $q$ : Elementary Charge; $N_d$ : Donor Concentration; $d_d$ : Donor Layer Thickness; 
    $\epsilon$ : Permittivity of the AlGaAs Barrier.}
    
\end{quote}

\vspace{2mm}

This quadratic dependence of $V_{off}$ on donor layer thickness
highlights the strong sensitivity of the threshold voltage to small
variations in etched depth. This relationship directly underpins the two
operational modes explored in this project.

\vspace{2mm}

\textbf{Depletion Mode (Semester~1):}  
The baseline devices fabricated in Semester~1 employed the full
epitaxial structure beneath the gate. Under these conditions, the
zero-bias depletion region does not extend to the heterointerface,
leaving the 2DEG populated ($V_{off} < 0$) and the device normally on,
such that a negative gate voltage is required to pinch off the channel
\cite{Mimura1980}.

\vspace{2mm}

\textbf{Enhancement Mode (Semester~2 Objective):}  
To achieve normally-off behaviour ($V_{off} > 0$), the donor layer must
be thinned until the depletion region fully intersects the 2DEG at zero
gate bias. For a donor concentration of \(1\times 10^{18}~\text{cm}^{-3}\), 
the critical donor thickness is approximately \(25~\text{nm}\) \cite{ivcv-hemt}; 
this finding by Lee \textit{et al.} serves as a well-grounded guide for the similar 
GaAs/AlGaAs material structure employed in this project. Recessing the gate to this depth shifts
the threshold voltage, such that a positive
gate bias is required to re-populate the channel and initiate
conduction \cite{Saitto2006}.

\vspace{2mm}

Together, these relationships demonstrate that achieving
enhancement-mode operation in GaAs/AlGaAs HEMTs depends critically on
precise control of the donor layer thickness beneath the gate, forming
the physical basis for the fabrication strategy developed in Chapter 5.
