\section{Overview of GaAs/AlGaAs HEMTs}

In the GaAs/AlGaAs HEMTs used in this project, the interface between the
GaAs channel layer and the overlying AlGaAs barrier produces a
discontinuity in the conduction band because the two materials possess
different bandgaps. This offset creates a potential well on the GaAs side of the junction, as
illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:band_bending}, which highlights the
conduction band discontinuity responsible for carrier confinement and
2DEG formation at the heterointerface.
Electrons supplied by silicon donors in the modulation doped AlGaAs
layer transfer into this well and accumulate to form a two dimensional
electron gas (2DEG) that serves as the conducting channel. The formation
of this spatially confined, high mobility 2DEG at the heterointerface is
the defining feature of the HEMT structure \cite{liu1999}.

\section{The GaAs/AlGaAs Heterostructure}

The HEMTs investigated in this project are based on a conventional
GaAs/AlGaAs heterostructure in which a sequence of epitaxial layers is
engineered to create and control a 2DEG. A schematic cross-section of the structure is shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:layer_structure}, highlighting the cap, donor, spacer and
channel layers, together with their relative thicknesses, that determine
the electrostatic control and transport behaviour of the device.

\vspace{2mm}
\input{figures/fig-layer-structure.tex}
\vspace{5mm}

At the surface of the structure lies a 10~nm thick, highly doped GaAs cap
layer. This cap provides a stable and chemically robust surface for
forming the Schottky gate contact, whilst also preventing oxidation of
the underlying AlGaAs. Its high conductivity reduces series resistance
in the access regions adjacent to the source and drain contacts, thereby
improving current injection into the channel.

\vspace{2mm}

Beneath the cap layer is a 40~nm thick, silicon doped AlGaAs donor layer.
Silicon donors in AlGaAs act as electron suppliers, with carriers
transferring into the GaAs channel as a result of the conduction band
discontinuity at the heterointerface. This approach is known as
modulation doping, as the dopants are spatially separated from the
channel rather than placed directly within it. Both the donor
concentration and its separation from the interface strongly influence
the resulting sheet carrier density of the 2DEG, and therefore the
threshold voltage and maximum drain current of the device.

\vspace{2mm}

A 15~nm thick undoped AlGaAs spacer layer separates the donor layer from
the GaAs channel. This layer is critical to device performance; by
physically isolating the 2DEG from the ionised silicon dopants, it
significantly suppresses Coulomb scattering \cite{Hirakawa1986}. This
spatial separation allows carriers to propagate through the channel with
exceptionally high mobility, which is a defining advantage of
GaAs/AlGaAs HEMTs. The effect is particularly pronounced at cryogenic
temperatures, where phonon scattering is minimised and impurity
scattering would otherwise dominate transport behaviour
\cite{Pfeiffer1989}. The spacer thickness therefore represents a
fundamental design trade off; increasing the spacer thickness improves
mobility but weakens electrostatic coupling between the gate and the
channel, reducing transconductance.

\section{HEMT Operating Principles}

The electrical behaviour of the GaAs/AlGaAs HEMT is governed by the field-effect
interaction between the Schottky gate and the 2DEG. When a metal gate is
deposited onto the semiconductor surface, a depletion region forms beneath it,
the depth of which is modulated by the applied gate voltage ($V_G$). This
modulation controls the sheet carrier density ($n_s$) in the channel and,
consequently, the drain current \cite{sze2021}.

To accurately describe this behaviour, particularly for the thin
heterostructures required for enhancement-mode operation, a refined
charge-control model is necessary. As derived by Lee et al. \cite{ivcv-hemt},
the relationship between the gate voltage and the channel charge is not solely
a function of the physical dielectric thickness, but must account for the
quantum mechanical distribution of electrons within the potential well.

\subsection{Transconductance and the Quantum Correction}

The device's transconductance ($g_m$)---its ability to convert a change in gate
voltage into a change in drain current---is fundamentally determined by the
gate-to-channel capacitance. In a classical parallel-plate model, this is
inversely proportional to the physical barrier thickness, $d$. However, the
electrons in the 2DEG do not reside strictly at the heterointerface; they are
distributed within the quantum well with a charge centroid located at a finite
distance from the interface.

Lee et al. introduce an "effective" gate-to-channel separation, defined as
$d + \Delta d$, where $\Delta d$ represents a quantum correction factor of
approximately $80~\text{\AA}$ ($8~\text{nm}$) \cite{ivcv-hemt}. This correction
modifies the transconductance parameter $\beta$ as follows:

\begin{equation}
    \beta = \frac{\epsilon \mu W}{(d + \Delta d)L}
\end{equation}

This relationship highlights a critical design trade-off for this project. While
recessing the gate (reducing $d$) significantly enhances electrostatic coupling,
the intrinsic separation $\Delta d$ imposes a fundamental limit on the
achievable transconductance. For the thick depletion-mode devices fabricated in
Semester 1 ($d \approx 50~\text{nm}$), the $8~\text{nm}$ correction is a minor
component. However, as the gate is recessed towards the target depth for
enhancement-mode operation ($\approx 25~\text{nm}$), $\Delta d$ becomes a
significant fraction of the total separation, limiting the extent to which $g_m$
can be scaled by thinning the barrier alone.

\subsection{Threshold Voltage and Mode Determination}

The distinction between depletion-mode (D-mode) and enhancement-mode (E-mode)
operation is determined by the balance between the built-in potential of the
Schottky gate and the thickness of the doped AlGaAs donor layer. According to
the analytical model \cite{ivcv-hemt}, the threshold voltage ($V_{off}$) is
dependent on the square of the donor layer thickness ($d_d$):

\begin{equation}
    V_{off} = \phi_b - \Delta E_c - \frac{q N_d d_d^2}{2\epsilon}
\end{equation}



This quadratic dependence ($V_{off} \propto d_d^2$) underscores the sensitivity
of the fabrication process; small variations in the recess etch depth result in
significant shifts in the threshold voltage. This physical relationship governs
the two distinct operational modes explored in this project:

\textbf{Depletion Mode (Semester 1):} The baseline devices fabricated in
Semester 1 utilised the full epitaxial structure, comprising the 10~nm GaAs cap
and the 40~nm AlGaAs donor layer. With a total thickness of
$d \approx 50~\text{nm}$, the depletion region from the Schottky gate does not
extend to the heterointerface at zero bias. Consequently, the 2DEG remains
populated ($V_{off} < 0$), and the device is "normally-on," requiring a negative
gate voltage to pinch off the channel \cite{Mimura1980}.

\textbf{Enhancement Mode (Semester 2 Goal):} To achieve normally-off behaviour
($V_{off} > 0$), the donor layer thickness must be reduced until the zero-bias
depletion region fully intersects the 2DEG. Based on the doping concentration of
$1 \times 10^{18}~\text{cm}^{-3}$ used in these wafers, Lee et al. suggest a
critical target thickness of approximately $25~\text{nm}$ ($250~\text{\AA}$)
\cite{ivcv-hemt}. By precisely recessing the gate to this depth, the built-in
potential is sufficient to deplete the channel at $V_G = 0$, requiring a
positive gate bias to re-populate the 2DEG and initiate conduction
\cite{Saitto2006}.
