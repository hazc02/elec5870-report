\section{Overview of GaAs/AlGaAs HEMTs}

In the GaAs/AlGaAs HEMTs used in this project, the interface between the
GaAs channel layer and the overlying AlGaAs barrier produces a
discontinuity in the conduction band due to the differing bandgaps of
the two materials. This offset creates a potential well on the GaAs
side of the junction, as illustrated in
Figure~\ref{fig:band_bending}, which is responsible for carrier
confinement and the formation of a two-dimensional electron gas
(2DEG) at the heterointerface.
Electrons supplied by silicon donors in the modulation-doped AlGaAs
layer transfer into this well and accumulate to form the 2DEG, which
serves as the conducting channel of the device.
The formation of this spatially confined, high-mobility channel is the
defining feature of the HEMT structure and underpins its superior
high-frequency and low-noise performance compared to bulk-channel
transistors \cite{liu1999}.

\section{The GaAs/AlGaAs Heterostructure}

The HEMTs investigated in this project are based on a conventional
GaAs/AlGaAs heterostructure in which a sequence of epitaxial layers is
engineered to create and control a 2DEG.
A schematic cross-section of the structure is shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:layer_structure}, highlighting the cap, donor, spacer,
and channel layers whose thicknesses and compositions govern the
electrostatic control and transport behaviour of the device.

\vspace{2mm}
\input{figures/fig-layer-structure.tex}
\vspace{5mm}

At the surface of the structure lies a $10~\text{nm}$ thick, highly doped
GaAs cap layer. This cap provides a chemically stable surface for
forming the Schottky gate contact whilst also protecting the underlying
AlGaAs from oxidation. Its high conductivity reduces series resistance
in the access regions beneath the source and drain contacts,
improving current injection into the channel.

\vspace{2mm}

Beneath the cap layer is a $40~\text{nm}$ thick silicon-doped AlGaAs
donor layer. Silicon donors act as electron suppliers, with carriers
transferring into the GaAs channel as a result of the conduction band
discontinuity at the heterointerface. This approach, known as
modulation doping, spatially separates the ionised dopants from the
conducting channel. Both the donor concentration and the layer thickness
strongly influence the resulting 2DEG sheet density and therefore the
threshold voltage and maximum drain current of the device.

\vspace{2mm}

A $15~\text{nm}$ thick undoped AlGaAs spacer layer separates the donor
layer from the GaAs channel. This spacer plays a critical role in device
performance by physically isolating the 2DEG from ionised dopants,
thereby suppressing Coulomb scattering \cite{Hirakawa1986}. This
separation enables exceptionally high electron mobility, particularly
at cryogenic temperatures where phonon scattering is reduced and
impurity scattering would otherwise dominate transport behaviour
\cite{Pfeiffer1989}. Increasing the spacer thickness improves mobility
but weakens electrostatic coupling between the gate and the channel;
this trade-off is a central consideration in heterostructure design.

\vspace{2mm}

In this project, the donor layer thickness and the resulting effective
separation between the gate and the channel represent the primary
controllable parameters through which the threshold voltage may be
engineered. The following section therefore focuses specifically on how
gate bias modulates channel charge and determines the operational mode
of the device.

\section{Gate Control of Channel Charge and Threshold Voltage}

The electrical behaviour of the GaAs/AlGaAs HEMT is governed by the
field-effect interaction between the Schottky gate and the 2DEG. When a
metal gate is deposited onto the semiconductor surface, a depletion
region forms beneath it; the depth of this region is modulated by the
applied gate voltage ($V_G$). This modulation controls the sheet carrier
density ($n_s$) in the channel and, consequently, the drain current
\cite{sze2021}.

For conventional depletion-mode devices with relatively thick barrier
layers, this behaviour can be approximated using a classical
parallel-plate charge-control model. However, as the gate-to-channel
separation is reduced towards the nanometre scale required for
enhancement-mode operation, additional physical effects must be
considered. In particular, the finite spatial extent of the 2DEG within
the quantum well modifies the effective electrostatic coupling between
the gate and the channel, motivating the refined charge-control
treatment introduced by Lee \textit{et al.} \cite{ivcv-hemt}.

\subsection{Finite Charge Distribution and Effective Gate Separation}

In an idealised classical model, the 2DEG is assumed to reside at an
infinitesimally thin sheet located precisely at the GaAs/AlGaAs
interface. In reality, electrons occupy a quantised triangular
potential well on the GaAs side of the heterointerface, resulting in a
finite charge distribution whose centroid is displaced from the
interface.

Lee \textit{et al.} account for this effect by introducing an effective
gate-to-channel separation of $d + \Delta d$, where $\Delta d$
represents a quantum-mechanical correction associated with the finite
extent of the electron wavefunction. For GaAs/AlGaAs HEMTs, this
correction is of order $80~\text{\AA}$ ($8~\text{nm}$) \cite{ivcv-hemt}.
Incorporating this correction modifies the effective gate capacitance
and may be expressed through the transconductance parameter $\beta$ as

\begin{equation}
    \beta = \frac{\epsilon \mu W}{(d + \Delta d)L}
\end{equation}

In the context of this project, $\Delta d$ does not represent a
fundamental performance limit; rather, it acts as an effective offset
that becomes increasingly relevant as the physical barrier thickness
$d$ is reduced through gate recessing. For the baseline depletion-mode
devices fabricated in Semester~1 ($d \approx 50~\text{nm}$), the quantum
correction constitutes a relatively small fraction of the total
separation. However, as the donor layer is thinned towards the
$\sim 25~\text{nm}$ regime required for enhancement-mode operation,
$\Delta d$ becomes a non-negligible contribution to the overall
electrostatic spacing. Its primary significance at this stage lies in
informing the practical target recess depth required to achieve a
positive threshold voltage, rather than in limiting transconductance
scaling.

\subsection{Threshold Voltage and Mode Determination}

The distinction between depletion-mode (D-mode) and enhancement-mode
(E-mode) operation is determined by the balance between the built-in
potential of the Schottky gate and the thickness of the doped AlGaAs
donor layer. Within the analytical framework presented by Lee
\textit{et al.} \cite{ivcv-hemt}, the threshold voltage ($V_{off}$) may
be expressed as

\begin{equation}
    V_{off} = \phi_b - \Delta E_c - \frac{q N_d d_d^2}{2\epsilon}
\end{equation}

where $\phi_b$ is the Schottky barrier height, $\Delta E_c$ is the
conduction band offset, $N_d$ is the donor concentration, and $d_d$ is
the donor layer thickness.

The quadratic dependence of $V_{off}$ on $d_d$ highlights the extreme
sensitivity of the threshold voltage to small variations in etched
depth. This relationship directly underpins the two operational modes
explored in this project:

\textbf{Depletion Mode (Semester~1):}  
The baseline devices fabricated in Semester~1 employed the full
epitaxial structure, comprising the $10~\text{nm}$ GaAs cap and the
$40~\text{nm}$ AlGaAs donor layer. With a total gate-to-channel
separation of approximately $50~\text{nm}$, the depletion region does
not intersect the 2DEG at zero gate bias. As a result, the channel
remains populated ($V_{off} < 0$), and the device is normally on,
requiring a negative gate voltage to achieve pinch-off
\cite{Mimura1980}.

\textbf{Enhancement Mode (Semester~2 Objective):}  
To achieve normally-off behaviour ($V_{off} > 0$), the donor layer must
be thinned until the zero-bias depletion region fully depletes the
2DEG. For the donor concentration of
$1 \times 10^{18}~\text{cm}^{-3}$ used in this project, Lee
\textit{et al.} identify a critical donor thickness of approximately
$25~\text{nm}$ ($250~\text{\AA}$) \cite{ivcv-hemt}. Recessing the gate to
this depth shifts the threshold voltage into the positive regime, such
that a positive gate bias is required to re-populate the channel and
initiate conduction \cite{Saitto2006}.

This strong dependence of threshold voltage on etched depth implies
that fabrication non-idealities affecting the effective recess depth,
such as surface oxidation and etch incubation effects, will directly
translate into device-to-device variability. These considerations
motivate the detailed examination of wet-etch behaviour and oxide
control strategies presented in the following fabrication chapter and
form a central focus of the enhancement-mode process development
outlined in Chapter~5.
