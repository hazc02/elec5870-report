\chapter{Electrical Characterisation and Analysis of Depletion-Mode Devices}

This chapter presents the electrical characterisation and critical analysis of
the depletion-mode GaAs/AlGaAs HEMT devices fabricated using the unrecessed
process described in Chapter~3. The primary objective of this analysis is to
establish a well-understood electrical baseline for threshold voltage,
transconductance, and gate leakage behaviour, against which the effects of
controlled gate recession may later be assessed.

\vspace{2mm}

Although devices of multiple lateral geometries were fabricated and
characterised, these variations are not treated here as primary research
variables. Instead, the range of gate lengths and source--drain separations
serves to confirm that key electrostatic parameters, most notably threshold
voltage, are governed predominantly by the vertical heterostructure and
Schottky gate properties rather than by lateral device dimensions. This
distinction is central to the subsequent investigation of enhancement-mode
operation, in which threshold voltage is expected to be strongly dependent on
recess depth.

\section{Measurement Setup}

Electrical measurements were carried out using a Keysight B1500A semiconductor
device parameter analyser under ambient laboratory conditions at room
temperature (\SI{20}{\degree C}). \cite{leedscleanroom_equipment}

\vspace{2mm}

Output characteristics were measured by sweeping the drain--source voltage
($V_{DS}$) at fixed gate biases to assess channel conduction and saturation
behaviour. Gate transfer characteristics were obtained by sweeping the
gate--source voltage ($V_G$) at a fixed drain bias chosen to ensure operation
within the saturation regime. From these measurements, the drain current
($I_D$), transconductance ($g_m$), and threshold voltage were extracted.

\vspace{2mm}

Channel resistance was determined from the low-bias linear region of the
output characteristics, where the device operates in the ohmic regime.
Transconductance was calculated numerically from the measured $I_D$--$V_G$
data using finite-difference methods. Identical biasing conditions and data
processing procedures were applied consistently across the full device set.

\vspace{2mm}

Electrical characteristics were averaged across the nominally identical
devices to obtain representative behaviour while suppressing device-to-device
variation. The spread between repeats was retained for later discussion of
process repeatability and its implications for gate recess sensitivity.

\section{Baseline Output Characteristics}

The output characteristics of the fabricated depletion-mode GaAs/AlGaAs HEMTs
were examined primarily to verify ohmic contact behaviour and to confirm
stable channel conduction prior to threshold-voltage analysis. Figure~\ref{fig:id_vds}
shows the measured $I_D$--$V_{DS}$ characteristics for a representative device
with a gate length of \SI{6}{\micro\metre}, averaged across the three nominally
identical devices fabricated at this geometry.

\vspace{2mm}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.75\linewidth]{figures/electrical_analysis/output_characteristics.png}
    \caption{Measured output characteristics ($I_D$--$V_{DS}$) of a
    representative depletion-mode GaAs/AlGaAs HEMT with a \SI{6}{\micro\metre}
    gate length. Curves are shown for multiple gate voltages, illustrating
    linear-region conduction and current saturation.}
    \label{fig:id_vds}
\end{figure}

At low drain--source voltages, the $I_D$ exhibits a clear linear
relationship with $V_{DS}$ for all applied gate biases, confirming ohmic
conduction and low-resistance source and drain contacts. This indicates that
the ohmic metallisation and annealing process was successful, and that contact
resistance does not dominate the measured device behaviour.

\vspace{2mm}

As $V_{DS}$ is increased, the devices transition smoothly into current
saturation, consistent with electrostatic pinch-off of the channel near the
drain. The systematic modulation of the saturation current with gate voltage
demonstrates effective gate control of the 2DEG and
confirms stable operation.

\section{Gate Transfer Characteristics and Threshold Voltage Behaviour}

The primary objective of this project is to understand and control threshold
voltage through gate recessing. It is therefore necessary to first establish
the baseline threshold behaviour of unrecessed devices.

\vspace{2mm}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.75\linewidth]{figures/electrical_analysis/gate_transfer_average.png}
    \caption{Averaged transfer characteristics ($I_D$--$V_G$) for all fabricated
    depletion-mode HEMTs, comprising five lateral geometries with three
    nominally identical devices per geometry.}
    \label{fig:id_vg_all}
\end{figure}

\vspace{2mm}

Figure~\ref{fig:id_vg_all} shows the averaged linear-scale gate transfer
characteristics for the full device set. Across all geometries, the transition
from the depleted regime to strong channel conduction occurs at
approximately the same gate voltage, yielding a threshold voltage of
%
\[
V_T \approx \SI{-0.9}{\volt}.
\]
%
No systematic shift in the turn-on point is observed, demonstrating that the
threshold voltage is effectively independent of lateral device geometry for
the unrecessed devices fabricated in this work.

\vspace{2mm}

This behaviour is consistent with the threshold-voltage model introduced in
Chapter~2 (Equation~2.2), in which $V_T$ is governed by the vertical
electrostatics beneath the Schottky gate. In the present devices, the gate is
formed on the GaAs cap layer, and the depletion field must extend through the
cap, the full AlGaAs donor layer, and the undoped spacer to fully deplete the
2DEG. Under these conditions, $V_T$ depends on parameters such as the Schottky
barrier height, donor charge density, and donor-layer thickness, while lateral
device dimensions do not appear explicitly in the first-order electrostatic
description.

\vspace{2mm}

Differences observed in the on-state current between devices are modest and
are attributed to lateral transport effects, including variations in channel
and access resistance arising from differences in gate length and
source--drain separation. These variations influence current drive but do not
alter the electrostatic condition required to deplete the channel, and
therefore do not affect the threshold voltage.

\vspace{2mm}

The key implication for this project is that the baseline threshold voltage is
set by the unrecessed vertical layer stack, yielding
$V_T \approx \SI{-0.9}{\volt}$ across all fabricated geometries. This result
confirms that threshold-voltage engineering in this technology must be
achieved by modifying the vertical electrostatics beneath the gate.
Controlled gate recession into the AlGaAs donor layer is therefore confirmed as an
appropriate process variable for shifting $V_T$ towards the positive regime.

\section{Gate Leakage Behaviour}

Gate leakage was examined to characterise the gate--semiconductor interface in
the unrecessed devices and to provide a reference for comparison with
recessed-gate devices fabricated in the subsequent phase of this project.
Figure~\ref{fig:ig_vds} presents the averaged gate leakage characteristics for
all fabricated devices, spanning the full range of lateral geometries within
the array.

\vspace{2mm}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{figures/electrical_analysis/gate_leakage_average_bulb_off.png}
    \caption{Measured gate leakage characteristics for unrecessed depletion-mode
    GaAs/AlGaAs HEMTs, averaged across nominally identical devices for each
    column in the array.}
    \label{fig:ig_vds}
\end{figure}

\vspace{2mm}

Across the full device set, the measured gate leakage currents are of similar
order of magnitude over the applied drain-bias range, with no strong or
systematic dependence on gate length or source--drain separation observed.

\vspace{2mm}

This behaviour suggests that gate leakage is governed primarily by the
gate–semiconductor interface, with any dependence on lateral device
dimensions falling below the resolution of the present measurements.

\vspace{2mm}
In the unrecessed geometry studied here, the Schottky gate is formed on the GaAs
cap layer, where a thin native oxide and associated surface states are
expected to be present. The small spread in leakage observed between columns
is therefore most plausibly attributed to local variations in interface
quality beneath the gate rather than to systematic electrostatic effects
arising from device layout.

\vspace{2mm}

In recessed devices, the Schottky gate will be
formed directly on the AlGaAs donor layer rather than on the GaAs cap,
introducing a fundamentally different gate--semiconductor interface 
\cite{Schweeger1994}. In GaAs/AlGaAs HEMT structures, Schottky contacts formed 
on AlGaAs are commonly reported to exhibit increased gate leakage compared to 
GaAs-based interfaces, reflecting the closer proximity of the donor layer to 
the gate, a reduced effective barrier width, and enhanced tunnelling currents 
\cite{Schweeger1994}. These effects are further influenced by differences in 
surface state density and band alignment at the metal--semiconductor 
interface \cite{Schweeger1994}.

\vspace{2mm}

The gate leakage behaviour observed in the unrecessed devices therefore
provides a physically meaningful reference condition. Any systematic change
in gate leakage observed following gate recession may be attributed primarily
to the modified gate--semiconductor interface and vertical electrostatics,
rather than to lateral geometry effects present in the unrecessed devices.
